One strategy, done with discipline.

No guessing, no chasing, no holding through earnings on a whim. You know the exit before the entry.

Structure
Bull call and bear put debit spreadsBull calls when the trend is up, bear puts when it breaks down. Either way, your risk is capped at the debit you pay.
Stop
About half the debitSet as a standing order the day the trade opens.
Target

Questions

Straight answers.

Do you trade your own alerts?

Yes. Every trade we recommend, we place ourselves in our own account at Schwab. The fill price on your alert is the price we actually got.

Do you show your losing trades?

Yes. Every closed trade goes on the record with its actual entry and exit fills, winners and losers alike. Losses are part of trading. Small, planned losses are how we stay in the game.

What is a bull call debit spread?

You buy one call and sell another at a higher strike, same expiry. It costs less than buying the call alone, and the most you can lose is what you paid to open it. The most you can make is the gap between the strikes minus that cost.

How much money do I need?

Spreads keep the cost per trade low, often a few hundred dollars per contract. Size every trade to your own account and never risk money you can't afford to lose.

How fast do the alerts arrive?

By email, the moment we act. You get the alert as we open or close a trade, then a confirmation with our actual fill prices, so you know exactly where we got in and out.

Where does the AI end and the human begin?

Our Python scanners and Claude do the searching, grading, pricing and checking, and a Bulkowski chart-pattern agent is in development. Mike Lesko reviews every setup and makes the final decision on every trade.

Can I get alerts in my own language?

Yes. Tell us your language when you sign up and your alerts and reports arrive translated. The numbers never change.

Can an autotrader place the trades for me?

Yes, through the autotrading services we work with. Ask us and we'll walk you through the setup.

Is this personal financial advice?

No. We publish our own trades and our reasons for them. What you trade, and how much, is always your decision.
